# Mayadeen R&D Charter

**Status:** Permanent research and development charter
**Effective date:** 2026-07-11
**Applies to:** Every current and future Mayadeen Event Intelligence Twin agent
**Approval owner:** Ahmed

This charter is the intellectual foundation for Mayadeen Event Intelligence Twin research and development. Every agent must read it before proposing research, architecture, technology, or product direction. It governs research and recommendations; it does not authorize production implementation.

## Current Product Boundary

The current product is an Arabic-first RTL spatial decision and operational exercise interface. It uses temporary demo data and a local baseline/scenario distinction. It must not be described as a live digital twin, verified readiness source, simulation engine, calibrated projection system, or source of operational truth.

Research may look ahead. Implementation must remain inside the approved roadmap and active sprint. No researched technology may be implemented without Ahmed's explicit approval. Agents must not restart completed work, create a new product phase, or expand the active sprint through research enthusiasm.

## Strategic Ambition

The long-term ambition is Mayadeen Event Intelligence OS: a specialized operating intelligence platform for major events. It should help an event:

- Observe itself.
- Understand its current condition.
- Predict future pressure, disruption, and risk.
- Explore alternative scenarios.
- Recommend traceable decisions.
- Coordinate human execution.
- Verify completion through evidence.
- Measure intervention impact.
- Learn across events.

The primary operating loop is:

`Observe -> Understand -> Simulate -> Recommend -> Approve -> Execute -> Verify -> Measure -> Learn`

The word `Simulate` in the loop refers to a future capability. A current scripted sequence remains a scripted scenario, exercise, briefing, or training artifact and must never be called a simulation.

## R&D Evaluation Standard

Every research memo, recommendation, and experiment must label its reasoning:

- **Evidence:** Directly supported by an existing Mayadeen artifact, measured test, approved source, or cited benchmark material.
- **Inference:** A reasoned conclusion from evidence that still needs validation.
- **Assumption:** A condition being taken as true for planning.
- **Proposal:** A candidate future direction, not a committed feature.
- **Unverified hypothesis:** A claim that requires a designed experiment.

Every recommendation must answer:

1. What operational problem does it solve?
2. Who uses it?
3. What decision improves?
4. What action results?
5. What measurable outcome changes?
6. What is the failure cost if it is wrong?
7. What is the simplest credible alternative?
8. Should Mayadeen build, buy, partner, or delay?
9. Which roadmap stage and approval gate apply?

## Differentiation Targets

The team must continuously test these potential differentiators against evidence and simpler alternatives:

1. **Event Ontology:** A unified operational language for major events.
2. **Spatial Decision Graph:** Decisions connected to location, people, dependencies, evidence, risk, and impact.
3. **Spatial Operational Memory:** What happened at every location across time.
4. **Operational Time Travel:** Reconstruct what was known, decided, approved, and observed at a past moment.
5. **Decision Impact Measurement:** Measure changes in flow, readiness, response time, queues, safety, and continuity.
6. **Scenario-to-Action:** Convert an approved scenario outcome into accountable operational actions.
7. **Digital-to-Physical Synchronization:** Keep command center, 2D, 3D, physical, field, and executive views aligned.
8. **Reusable Event Operating Model:** Carry lessons and operating knowledge into the next event.
9. **Predictive Readiness:** Move from static percentages to dependency-aware forecasts.
10. **Cross-Event Learning:** Identify recurring risks, bottlenecks, supplier failures, and successful responses.

These are research targets, not promises that the current product already provides them.

## Product Standard

Mayadeen must not become:

- A beautiful 3D viewer.
- A passive dashboard.
- A digital twin without operational action.
- A collection of disconnected modules.
- A scripted scenario falsely described as simulation.
- A command center without evidence and accountability.
- A physical model used only for presentation.
- A technically complex product with weak adoption.
- A platform dependent on one vendor or proprietary format.
- A product that forces every task into 3D.

Mayadeen must become a spatial operational intelligence system that helps event leadership understand conditions, explore future states, make traceable decisions, coordinate execution, verify outcomes, and learn continuously.

## R&D Operating Principles

1. Evidence before excitement.
2. Operational value before technical novelty.
3. No technology tourism.
4. Evaluate build, buy, partner, and delay for every major capability.
5. Prefer open architecture: OpenUSD, GLB/glTF, 3D Tiles, standard APIs, event-driven contracts, and replaceable adapters.
6. Respect stage discipline; research does not advance the roadmap.
7. No uncontrolled implementation; Ahmed must explicitly approve technical experiments and researched technology adoption.
8. Compare every advanced idea with a 2D map, checklist, spreadsheet, standard dashboard, or manual procedure.
9. Keep 2D, 3D, lists, timelines, and projection complementary; do not force every task into 3D.
10. Preserve current claim discipline: no operational claim without source and status classification, no readiness claim without owner and evidence, no route claim without approved geometry and authority, no simulation claim for a scripted scenario, and no projection-calibration claim for a visual preset.

## Approved Six-Stage Roadmap

1. **Stage 1:** Interactive spatial model and projection foundation.
2. **Stage 2:** Spatial storytelling and executive presentation.
3. **Stage 3:** Operational digital twin.
4. **Stage 4:** Scenario and simulation engine.
5. **Stage 5:** Decision and field-execution center.
6. **Stage 6:** Physical intelligent interface.

Live data, cameras, sensors, counters, crowd-density feeds, transport feeds, and operational integrations occur only after the core product is mature and ready for connection. This charter does not start the next product stage.

## Daily R&D Workflow

Each agent produces one focused memo per research day. Shallow idea volume is not a success measure. The memo format is defined in `docs/daily-rd/README.md`.

The Lead Integrator consolidates the memos into one executive report containing:

1. Executive summary.
2. Most important global technology reviewed.
3. Strongest Mayadeen opportunity.
4. Most dangerous assumption.
5. Highest-value recommendation.
6. Technology not recommended and why.
7. Build/buy/partner insight.
8. Decisions requiring Ahmed.
9. Tomorrow's research priorities.
10. Impact on the six-stage roadmap.

Every memo and consolidation must identify whether an item is `Critical now`, `Recommended soon`, `Strategic later`, or `Not recommended`.

## Governance and Approval

Before any technology, vendor, data source, workflow engine, sensor, camera, simulation system, physical interface, or spatial-computing capability is implemented:

1. A focused memo must exist.
2. The simplest credible alternative must be documented.
3. Build/buy/partner/delay must be evaluated.
4. Dependencies, failure cost, adoption risk, procurement risk, and reversibility must be stated.
5. The target roadmap stage must be named.
6. Ahmed must explicitly approve the technical experiment or implementation.

Until those gates are met, the item remains research, proposal, or unverified hypothesis. Ambition must be demonstrated by better decisions, stronger evidence, faster response, safer execution, measurable impact, reusable intelligence, Arabic-first usability, and open scalable architecture - not by feature accumulation.
